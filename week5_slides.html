A PBMC dataset, two tiny clusters, two decisions

Sammi Baudin
Data owner: Dr. Ravi Menon, immunologist

The data

2,700human PBMCs (cells)
13,714genes
8clusters, 0–7 (leiden)
  • PBMC = peripheral blood mononuclear cells: mainly lymphocytes and monocytes isolated from blood.
  • Blood collected in a university hospital lab. The core facility isolated the cells, ran single-cell RNA-seq and returned a processed file: clusters, a 2-D map (UMAP) and summary numbers.
  • The file holds log-normalised expression, raw UMI counts and per-cell quality fields (n_genes, total_counts, pct_mito).
  • No methods sheet was provided. The owner did not know the file's layout, what processing had been done, or whether the “genes/cell” summary was a mean or a median.

The owner trusts the facility's numbers most and the biological meaning of the numbered groups least, because nobody has identified them.— interview, paraphrased

Why the question matters: cleaning up before a figure

CLUSTER 613 cells · ~350 genes/cell · 1.6% mito
“Is it safe to delete?”

He was about to bin it as dead cells or empty droplets, based only on the low gene count, without looking at what the cells express.

CLUSTER 710 cells · ~2,363 genes/cell
“Should the next sequencing run go to this cluster?”

That spends facility time and budget. Yes only if the cluster has a clear, likely blood-cell identity (T, B, NK or monocyte); otherwise leave it out of the figure.

CoherentSeveral genes from the same lineage across most cells, e.g. CD3D/E + TRBC
MixedStrong programmes that don't fit together in the same cells, e.g. T-cell and monocyte
Generic / junkMostly mitochondrial, ribosomal, housekeeping or stress genes

Confidence is stated first
(project rule)
CLUSTER 6CONFIDENCE: MODERATE
Don't delete. Keep it for review.

It is not blank: the cells differ from each other, one cell has high depth, and the top markers PPBP/PF4 match the owner's own platelet-contamination clue. What the cells are is still unresolved.

CLUSTER 7CONFIDENCE: LOW–MODERATE
No. Don't prioritise the next run.

Its depth is high, but the T, B, NK and monocyte programmes overlap in the same cells, and the top markers are generic. This fits the owner's definition of “mixed or uninformative”.

One honest limit

Both answers rest on 10 and 13 cells. Cluster 7 is the least stable cluster (some cells stay with it in only 21% of runs), and my doublet flag is a screening rule, not a validated classifier. So this is enough evidence to hold back a decision, but not enough to name either cluster's identity.
